Learning the Structure: What is Different about Interlock?

In order to fully enjoy interlock fabric, one must know how it is made.

A typical jersey product can be spotted as having a distinct front and back. The knit on the front of it is in a V-shape and the back of it has horizontal ribbed knit. It is also more likely to roll along the edges making it extremely harder to handle and sew.

However, interlock fabric is made with 2 sets of needles knitting at the same time and opposite each other. Simply, it is a two-layered jersey cloth put together into one.

This results in:

  • One richer and more solid framework.
  • Smooth and corresponding surfaces on either side.
  • No "right" or "wrong" side
  • Another one is a naturally opaque finish (no problems with transparency).

Structural Advantage

Interlock does the same thing due to this balanced structure, it retains its shape remarkably well. It offers a structured but gentle touch, that is, the difference between an ordinary piece of clothing and a high-quality product.

The Ultimate Fiber Blend: Lycra + Polyester.

The performance is determined by the fibers whereas the knit structure serves as the base. This is a polyester and lycra combination that is the true strength of this fabric.

Polyester: Strength and Characteristics.

The current polyester is not the old mindset of the dumpy synthetic material. Nowadays, it is performance-engineered.

Key advantages include:

  • Moisture-wicking (hydrophobic nature): It pulls away moisture on the body, and then it evaporates fast.
  • Quick-drying: Sportswear and activewear.
  • Wrinkle resistance: Ideally suited to export and uniform use.
  • Color retention: Does not fade away easily even after numerous washing and sun exposure.

To exporters, this implies that clothes are delivered to the stores in a condition of being ready to retail without ironing or loss of quality.

Lycra: Stretch with Recovery

Lycra (or elastane or spandex) provides stretch and strength to the dress.

It is as easy to work, and as effective as:

  • Has stretch to ensure comfort and mobility.
  • Makes sure that the fabric is put back to its original shape.
  • Stops sagging in stressful places such as knees and elbows.

Garments lose their structure with the passage of time without the use of lycra. They do this with it, so that they can look high-quality and well-fitted all the way across their lifecycle.
